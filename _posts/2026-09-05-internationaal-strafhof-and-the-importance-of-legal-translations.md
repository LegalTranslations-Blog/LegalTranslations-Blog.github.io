When cases reach the internationaal strafhof (International Criminal Court), every word counts. In an environment where international contracts, notarial deeds, and official documents cross legal systems and languages, the stakes are high. For Belgium’s law firms, corporations, and public institutions involved in cross-border matters, the accuracy, compliance, and confidentiality of legal translations are not just priorities—they are imperatives.

## Why Precision Matters in Legal Translations

Legal documents, whether presented before the internationaal strafhof or in local courts, require precise language. Ambiguities or errors can lead to costly misinterpretations in international contracts or even jeopardize a case. Certified legal translators are trained to reflect the exact meaning, intent, and nuance of the original text, ensuring that translations stand up to scrutiny.

**Risks of Mistranslation:**
- **Invalid Contracts:** A single mistranslated clause can invalidate international agreements.
- **Legal Sanctions:** Inaccurate translations of legalizations & apostilles may cause documents to be rejected by authorities.
- **Compliance Risks:** Regulatory non-compliance due to translation errors can result in fines or legal action.

## The Role of Sworn and Certified Legal Translators

Sworn translations are essential for official documents such as notarial deeds and court submissions. Only sworn translators can provide certified translations that are recognized by Belgian courts and international bodies like the internationaal strafhof. This certification assures all parties that the translation is accurate, complete, and legally valid.

Professional legal translation services offer:

- **Confidentiality:** Sensitive information is protected throughout the translation process.
- **Accuracy:** Every term is carefully chosen to reflect the original document’s legal force.
- **Reliability:** Certified legal translators are bound by professional codes of conduct.
- **Error Correction:** Rigorous proofreading and review processes prevent misinterpretations.
